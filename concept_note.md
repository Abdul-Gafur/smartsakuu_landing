SmartSakuu Business Concept & Case
The AI-Powered Operating System for Schools and Educational Institutions in Africa
1. Business Concept
SmartSakuu is an AI-powered operating system that brings school management, teaching, learning and student support into one connected platform.
Schools already use different tools to manage admissions, attendance, fees, examinations, academic records, communication and learning. Students may also use separate learning platforms, past-question applications, tutoring tools and revision resources.
The problem is that these systems rarely understand each other.
SmartSakuu brings them together around one continuous school and student record.
A learner can move through:
Admission → Enrollment → Attendance → Teaching → Assessment → Examination → Learning → Revision → Academic Support → Graduation
without their educational history being fragmented across unrelated systems.
The same platform gives the school the tools required to operate the institution while giving students a digital learning environment that already understands their academic progress.
SmartSakuu connects:
School Management + Student Records + Attendance + Fees + HR + Examinations + Assessments + LMS + Curriculum + Teacher Tools + AI Tutor + Parent Engagement + Payments + Reporting + Institutional Knowledge + AI
The result is a school that operates more efficiently and students who receive learning support based on what they are actually learning, where they are performing well and where they need additional support.
 
2. The Problem SmartSakuu Addresses
Schools Need Better Ways to Connect Operations, Learning and Student Progress
Schools are expected to manage increasingly complex operations while also improving the learning outcomes of every student.
The challenge is that the information required to do both is often distributed across different systems, departments and people.
A school may have:
•	a school management system;
•	an attendance system;
•	an examination and grading process;
•	an accounting or payment platform;
•	an LMS;
•	learning resources;
•	past-question platforms;
•	communication tools;
•	spreadsheets;
•	physical records;
•	teacher observations.
Each system captures part of what is happening.
The school management system knows the student's academic and administrative history.
The examination system knows how the student performed.
The LMS knows what the learner studied and practised.
The teacher understands what happened in the classroom.
Attendance provides information about participation in school.
Parents hold additional context about the learner outside school.
When these records remain disconnected, schools can see individual events without easily understanding the complete learner.
A student may receive 48% in Mathematics, for example.
That result alone does not explain whether the student:
•	has struggled with algebra for several terms;
•	performs well in geometry but poorly in equations;
•	attends school consistently;
•	completes learning activities;
•	needs additional foundational support;
•	requires more practice;
•	previously received remedial support;
•	is improving or continuing to decline.
The information needed to answer those questions may already exist somewhere in the school.
The challenge is bringing it together early enough to guide action.
UNICEF Ghana's Education Budget Brief 2025 reports that more than 60% of children fail to achieve basic literacy and numeracy by the end of primary school.
The same analysis notes that Ghana's approximately 11.6 expected years of schooling translate into only about 5.7 learning-adjusted years, demonstrating that access to schooling does not automatically translate into equivalent learning.
UNICEF identifies foundational learning as a major education priority and highlights challenges including teacher capacity, infrastructure and the limited use of effective digital tools.
The need also extends beyond the early grades.
UNICEF Ghana has emphasized that foundational learning difficulties can continue into later stages of education, including JHS, SHS and TVET, meaning students may progress through the education system while still carrying unresolved learning gaps.
This makes early identification, continued assessment, remedial support and personalized learning increasingly important.
UNICEF's Work Also Highlights the Importance of Connected Learning Data
UNICEF's work with Ghana's Differentiated Learning Plus (DL+) programme demonstrates the importance of understanding students according to their actual learning levels and continuously tracking their progress.
The programme combines:
•	targeted instruction;
•	structured teaching support;
•	regular assessment;
•	learning-level differentiation;
•	real-time tracking of learner progression through digital systems.
UNICEF has also documented a practical challenge that closely reflects the problem SmartSakuu addresses.
In its work on data-driven decision-making in Ghana's education system, UNICEF described existing learning-data processes as manual and fragmented, making it harder for teachers, school leaders and education authorities to obtain timely information and respond to learning gaps.
The response included consolidating assessment information and providing real-time visibility into learner progression so educators could identify areas requiring attention and make better-informed interventions.
These findings reinforce an important principle:
Improving learning outcomes requires more than collecting data. Schools need to connect the information they already collect, understand what it says about each learner and turn it into timely academic support.
SmartSakuu applies this principle at the level of the individual school and student.
It connects school operations, academic records, assessments, curriculum progress and digital learning so that the same information used to run the institution also helps teachers, students, parents and school leaders understand learning progress.
The goal is straightforward:
Help schools operate more efficiently while giving every learner a better chance to receive the right support, learning resources and preparation at the right time.
Evidence basis: UNICEF Ghana Education Budget Brief 2025; UNICEF Ghana's Differentiated Learning Plus programme; UNICEF Ghana's work on data-driven decision-making and foundational learning.
 
3. The SmartSakuu Experience
The easiest way to understand SmartSakuu is to follow a student through a normal school day and academic term.
Morning
A student arrives at school and taps their Smart Student ID against the school attendance scanner.
Their attendance is recorded automatically in SmartSakuu.
The school immediately has an updated attendance record, and where configured, parents can receive attendance notifications.
The attendance record becomes part of the student's wider school history.
It is useful information, but SmartSakuu does not assume that attendance alone explains academic performance.
A student may attend every lesson and still struggle academically.
The strength of SmartSakuu comes from combining attendance with the many other records that describe the learner.
 
During Teaching
Before entering the classroom, a teacher opens SmartSakuu.
The teacher selects:
•	class;
•	subject;
•	strand;
•	sub-strand;
•	curriculum indicator;
•	lesson duration;
•	available teaching resources;
•	relevant learner context.
For Ghanaian schools, this can be aligned directly with the GES/NaCCA curriculum.
SmartSakuu assists the teacher in preparing:
•	lesson plans;
•	lesson notes;
•	schemes of learning;
•	teaching activities;
•	learner activities;
•	assignments;
•	quizzes;
•	teaching resources;
•	differentiated activities.
The teacher reviews and adapts the generated material before using it.
Because teaching happens inside the same platform that contains academic and learning information, SmartSakuu can progressively help teachers prepare lessons with a better understanding of how the class is performing.
If a large proportion of the class struggled with fractions in the previous assessment, the teacher can see that information while preparing the next lesson.
The technology therefore supports the teacher's work before, during and after instruction.
 
After Assessments and Examinations
Teachers enter or upload approved assessment and examination results.
SmartSakuu manages:
•	continuous assessment;
•	class tests;
•	assignments;
•	examination scores;
•	grading;
•	totals;
•	averages;
•	report cards;
•	subject performance;
•	class performance;
•	term-to-term performance;
•	academic history.
A score becomes more useful when SmartSakuu connects it to everything else the school already knows about the learner.
Consider a student who scores 48% in Mathematics.
The number alone tells the school the result.
SmartSakuu can place that result beside:
•	a previous Mathematics score of 61%;
•	repeated weakness in algebra;
•	quiz performance;
•	curriculum mastery;
•	learning activity inside the LMS;
•	past examination performance;
•	teacher observations;
•	relevant attendance information;
•	previous academic interventions.
The student may have 96% or even 100% attendance and still be struggling.
In that situation, attendance is clearly not the primary problem.
The school can instead see that the learner repeatedly struggles with algebra, performs poorly on related quizzes and has not mastered the underlying concepts.
SmartSakuu helps the school move from:
“The student scored 48%.”
to:
“The student is struggling with these specific concepts, this has persisted over time, and these are the areas where additional learning support is required.”
That is the intelligence created when school management and learning systems work together.

5. Personalized Learning Based on Actual School Performance
Students do not all struggle with the same topics.
A general learning platform may give every student the same content.
SmartSakuu has more context.
Suppose a learner is strong in geometry but repeatedly performs poorly in algebra.
SmartSakuu can identify that pattern across:
•	classroom assessments;
•	examinations;
•	LMS quizzes;
•	exercises;
•	previous terms;
•	curriculum progress.
The student's learning environment can then recommend:
Algebra → Linear Equations → Guided Lesson → Worked Examples → Targeted Practice → Mastery Quiz
Another student in the same class may receive a different learning recommendation.
The goal is to direct each learner's time toward the areas where it can make the greatest difference.
A student could see:
You are doing well in Geometry. Your recent assessments and practice show that Linear Equations need more attention. Complete these two lessons, practise these questions and attempt the mastery test afterwards.
SmartSakuu continuously updates this understanding as the learner studies and completes additional assessments.
 
6. AI Tutor With Educational Context
Students also have access to an AI-powered learning assistant.
The tutor works within the student's educational environment.
It can assist with:
•	explanations;
•	worked examples;
•	guided problem solving;
•	revision;
•	practice questions;
•	concept clarification;
•	examination preparation.
Because the tutor exists inside SmartSakuu, it can use the educational context available to the student.
A learner struggling with algebra can receive algebra-focused explanations and practice.
A learner repeatedly missing comprehension questions can receive additional comprehension exercises.
A WASSCE candidate can focus revision on subjects and topics where their performance indicates additional preparation is required.
The purpose of the AI tutor is to give students access to additional academic support between lessons, after school and during examination preparation.

Many students prepare using textbooks, past questions, extra classes and different online resources.
SmartSakuu brings examination preparation into the student's existing learning environment.
Students can access:
•	BECE past questions;
•	WASSCE past questions;
•	subject-based question banks;
•	topic-based questions;
•	timed practice;
•	mock examinations;
•	guided solutions;
•	revision plans;
•	AI tutoring;
•	performance analysis.
SmartSakuu can connect examination preparation to the student's previous school performance.
Instead of treating every topic equally, the platform can help learners prioritize areas requiring additional attention.
For example:
WASSCE Mathematics Preparation
Strong areas:
•	Geometry
•	Statistics
Needs additional work:
•	Algebra
•	Simultaneous Equations
•	Probability
Recommended preparation:
1.	Complete Algebra Revision Module
2.	Attempt 20 targeted past questions

5.	Reassess topic mastery
The learner therefore prepares using both examination resources and their own academic history.
 
8. Learn and Earn
SmartSakuu introduces positive incentives into the learning experience.
Students earn points for meaningful academic activity such as:
•	completing lessons;
•	completing assignments;
•	improving quiz performance;
•	maintaining learning consistency;
•	completing revision plans;
•	improving weak topics;
•	reaching learning milestones;
•	completing examination preparation activities.
Points can be converted into approved incentives such as:
•	airtime;
•	internet data bundles;
•	educational materials;
•	school rewards;
•	partner-sponsored benefits.
The aim is to reward progress, consistency and improvement.
A student should not receive rewards simply for clicking through content.
The reward system should remain connected to meaningful learning behaviour.
For example:
Complete your Algebra revision path and improve your mastery score from 42% to 65% → Earn 250 SmartSakuu Points.
The student sees an immediate connection between effort, progress and reward.

14. Student Performance Becomes a Shared Responsibility
Poor academic performance rarely has one universal cause.
Different students struggle for different reasons.
One learner may have poor attendance.
Another may attend every class but have weak foundational knowledge.
Another may understand lessons but practise too little.
Another may repeatedly struggle with one curriculum area.
Another may need a different explanation or additional teacher support.
SmartSakuu gives schools a broader picture by connecting available educational evidence.
This can include:
Assessments + Examination History + LMS Activity + Curriculum Progress + Topic Mastery + Teacher Context + Attendance + Previous Intervention
The school can investigate the likely reason instead of assuming the cause.
Teachers, parents and students can then respond to the same learning problem from different roles.
