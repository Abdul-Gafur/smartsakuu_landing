The problem

A school sees the score. But not always the story behind it.

Admissions, fees, examinations, attendance and learning often sit in separate systems. Staff have to piece a learner’s story together before they can understand what is happening. By then, the term may be over.

The number gives the result. It doesn’t explain a persistent algebra gap, too little practice, or another pattern that needs attention.

There is more to this learner’s story.

Topic mastery

Learning activity
Only 2 of 12 algebra practice tasks attempted.Practice activity lives in another system.
Attendance context

Connect the evidence early enough to respond to the actual learning gap.The next step stays unclear until someone collects the evidence by hand.

Why this matters

Every learning gap deserves an earlier response.

Ghana’s learning challenge makes timely insight urgent. Connecting what a school already knows can help teachers see who needs support, and where to start.

Institutional memory

The school’s knowledge should survive a handover.

Student history, learning progress, fee records and previous interventions remain with the institution when staff or leadership change.

  • Establish the baseline

    Keep assessment results connected to the learner.

  • Remember what was tried

    Record targeted support for persistent learning gaps.

  • Build on what the school knows

    Follow progress and outcomes across terms and years.
